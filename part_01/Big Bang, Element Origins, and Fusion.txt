It breaks down the formation of an ionic solid from its elements into a series of steps with known enthalpy changes.\n\n**Example: Born-Haber Cycle for NaCl**\nThe overall reaction is the formation of NaCl from its elements:\n$$\n\\text{Na}_{(s)} + \\frac{1}{2}\\text{Cl}_{2(g)} \\rightarrow \\text{NaCl}_{(s)} \\quad \\Delta H_f^\\circ\n$$\n\nThe cycle consists of the following steps:\n1.  **Sublimation of Sodium:** $$\\text{Na}_{(s)} \\rightarrow \\text{Na}_{(g)}$$ (Enthalpy of sublimation, $$\\Delta H_{sub}$$)\n2.  **Ionization of Sodium:** $$\\text{Na}_{(g)} \\rightarrow \\text{Na}^+_{(g)} + e^-$$ (First ionization energy, $$IE_1$$)\n3.  **Dissociation of Chlorine:** $$\\frac{1}{2}\\text{Cl}_{2(g)} \\rightarrow \\text{Cl}_{(g)}$$ (Half the bond dissociation energy, $$\\frac{1}{2}\\Delta H_{diss}$$)\n4.  **Electron Affinity of Chlorine:** $$\\text{Cl}_{(g)} + e^- \\rightarrow \\text{Cl}^-_{(g)}$$ (Electron affinity, $$EA$$)\n5.  **Formation of the Lattice:** $$\\text{Na}^+_{(g)} + \\text{Cl}^-_{(g)} \\rightarrow \\text{NaCl}_{(s)}$$ (Lattice enthalpy, but as formation, so it\u0027s negative, $$-\\Delta H_{mrežna}$$)\n\nAccording to Hess\u0027s Law, the sum of the steps must equal the overall enthalpy of formation:\n$$\n\\Delta H_f^\\circ \u003d \\Delta H_{sub} + IE_1 + \\frac{1}{2}\\Delta H_{diss} + EA - \\Delta H_{mrežna}\n$$\nFrom this, the lattice enthalpy can be calculated.\n\n## [[Enthalpy of Solution (ΔH_topilna)]]\nThis is the enthalpy change when one mole of a substance dissolves in a solvent.
