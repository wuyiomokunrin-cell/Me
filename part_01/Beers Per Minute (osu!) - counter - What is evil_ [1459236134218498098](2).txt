[09/01/2026 8:24 pm] rabusu
Here it is:

In regard to evolution, every strategy that prioritizes short-term survival greatly endangers you in the long term (game theory). However, perfect stability is not always an option for various reasons. For the simplest of lifeforms that do not possess the ability to make conscious decisions, it's just up to chance, really. From the very beginning, it's unavoidable to live in a world without predation. The world of life and evolution is not separate from the one of laws of physics that just simply do their job.

In the realm of conscious thought, where it's possible to evaluate the importance of cooperation, it becomes evident that a perfect world with no conflicts would be ideal, not necessarily even for everyone's sake, but for your own. 

However, that is not the world that we live in. The accessible resources being finite simply do not allow for that. If I share a meal with you, it's possible that we will both have to die out of hunger since it might not be enough after we split. The competition for resources becomes unavoidable, and someone will suffer in the end no matter what.

Therefore, we should consider evil as only unjustified behavior or, in other words, not provoked out of necessity towards other individuals. Everything that exists simply for the sake of introducing entropy towards the system or does so not out of necessity.

However, the line of justification becomes increasingly blurry in certain situations. People will try to twist the meaning of "necessary". And that is the core problem with defining objective evil. Depending on the circumstances, the very same deeds quickly lose their "evil" label even within the same cultures. We can objectively define what should be considered evil. The algorithm to arrive at the final conclusion. However, the outcome will never stay the same depending on the situation because one of the parameters is akin to a random number.
